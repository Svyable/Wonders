Method, limits and sources

How to read this model

This is a hand-built reconstruction, not a survey. The three pyramids are placed from published coordinates and drawn to their original base lengths and heights. Temples, causeways, cemeteries, quarries and the workers’ town follow published site plans, but their buildings are simplified blocks. Terrain is a smoothed model of the plateau’s slope, not a measured elevation grid.

People and worker numbers. The 20,000–30,000 figure is an estimate for the broad Fourth Dynasty construction workforce, not an annual census. Merer’s logs describe the activities of one delivery team. Contemporary surviving documents do not support precise animated staffing curves. The “People & civilization” panel separates documented evidence, scholarly estimates and interpretive reconstructions.

Dates follow the conventional chronology (Khufu c. 2589–2566 BCE). Egyptologists’ estimates differ by several decades. Building progress inside each reign is interpolated; the construction ramps show one plausible lower ramp and leave the unknown upper system out.

Interiors use published dimensions for Khufu’s and Khafre’s passages and chambers. Menkaure’s lower chambers and the Osiris Shaft are simplified. Wall textures, lighting and sarcophagus details are illustrative.

Three levels of evidence are kept apart by colour in X-ray view. Gold spaces have been entered or excavated. Cyan spaces were detected by peer-reviewed scanning and have not been entered, so their shapes are approximate. Violet marks claims without accepted evidence, including the 2025 radar claim under Khafre and the Atlantis and “Hall of Records” legends. They are shown so you can see what is being claimed, and they are off by default.

Before the pyramids. No excavation has found an earlier civilisation at Giza. Phoenician culture arose more than a thousand years after the Fourth Dynasty; Phoenicians came to Egypt as traders and saw the monuments as antiquities.

One correction to the source plan: Khafre’s causeway runs along the south side of the Sphinx enclosure, and the Sphinx is placed about 60 m north-west of where the flat plan drew it.
